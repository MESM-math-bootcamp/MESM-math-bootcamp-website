    Percent & ppm conversions

    1) Convert 250 ppm to a percentage.

    Solution

    Given

    • 250 ppm
    • \(1\% = 10{,}000\) ppm

    Step 1: Divide by 10,000 to convert ppm to percent

    \[ 250 \text{ ppm} = \frac{250}{10{,}000}\% = 0.025\% \]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{0.025\%} \]

    2) A water sample is 0.015% contaminant by mass. Express this in ppm.

    Solution

    Given

    • 0.015% contaminant by mass
    • \(1\% = 10{,}000\) ppm

    Step 1: Multiply by 10,000 to convert percent to ppm

    \[ 0.015\% \times 10{,}000 = 150 \text{ ppm} \]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{150 \text{ ppm}} \]

    3) A 500 g soil sample is 2.4% organic matter by mass, and separately contains 380 ppm of a heavy metal. What is the ratio of organic matter mass to heavy metal mass in the sample?

    Heavy metal mass: \[ 500\ \text{g} \times 380\ \text{ppm} = 500\ \text{g} \times 0.000380 = 0.19\ \text{g} \]

    Ratio: \[ \frac{12\ \text{g}}{0.19\ \text{g}} \approx 63.2 : 1 \]

    Solution

    Given

    • Sample mass = 500 g
    • Organic matter = 2.4% by mass
    • Heavy metal = 380 ppm by mass

    Step 1: Find the mass of organic matter

    \[ 500\ \text{g} \times 2.4\% = 500 \text{g} * 2.4 / 100 = 12\ \text{g} \]

    Step 2: Find the mass of heavy metal

    \[ 500\ \text{g} \times 380\ \text{ppm} = 500\ \text{g} \times 0.000380 = 0.19\ \text{g} \]

    Step 3: Take the ratio

    \[ \frac{12\text{ g}}{0.19\text{ g}} \approx 63.2 \]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{63.2} \]

    Unit conversions

    4) Convert 2,300 mL to liters.

    Solution

    Given

    • Volume = 2,300 mL

    Step 1: Convert mL to L

    \[ 2{,}300\text{ mL} = \frac{2{,}300}{1000} = 2.3\text{ L} \]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{2.3\text{ L}} \]

    5) Convert an area of 5.4 km² to m².

    Solution

    Given

    • Area = 5.4 km²

    Step 1: Square the linear conversion factor

    \[ 1\text{ km} = 1{,}000\text{ m} \implies 1\text{ km}^2 = (1{,}000)^2\text{ m}^2 = 10^6\text{ m}^2 \]

    Step 2: Apply the factor

    \[ 5.4\text{ km}^2 = 5.4 \times 10^6\text{ m}^2 \]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{5{,}400{,}000\text{ m}^2} \]

    6) A reservoir has average depth 12 m and surface area 1.8 km². Using Volume = depth × area, find the volume in m³.

    Solution

    Given

    • Depth = 12 m
    • Surface area = 1.8 km²

    Step 1: Convert area to square meters

    \[ 1.8\text{ km}^2 = 1.8 \times (1{,}000)^2 = 1.8 \times 10^6\text{ m}^2 \]

    Step 2: Calculate the volume

    \[ V = \text{depth} \times \text{area} \]

    \[ V = 12 \times 1.8\times10^6 \]

    \[ V = 2.16 \times 10^7\text{ m}^3 \]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{21{,}600{,}000\text{ m}^3} \]

    7) A saline stock is 6 g/L. Express this in mg/mL.

    Solution

    Given

    • Concentration = 6 g/L

    Step 1: Convert grams to milligrams

    \[ 6\text{ g} = 6{,}000\text{ mg} \]

    Step 2: Convert liters to milliliters

    \[ 1\text{ L} = 1{,}000\text{ mL} \]

    Step 3: Divide to get the new concentration

    \[ \frac{6{,}000\text{ mg}}{1{,}000\text{ mL}} = 6\text{ mg/mL} \]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{6\text{ mg/mL}} \]

    8) One microgram (μg) is equivalent to \(1/10^6\) grams and one meter cubed (m³) is equivalent to 1000 liters. A pollutant concentration is 12 μg/mL. Convert this to kg/m³.

    Solution

    Given

    • Concentration = 12 μg/mL
    • \(1\ \mu\text{g} = 10^{-6}\text{ g}\) and \(1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ L}\)

    Step 1: Find a conversion factor from micrograms to kilograms

    \[ 1\ \mu\text{g} = 10^{-6}\text{ g} = 10^{-9}\text{ kg} \implies \frac{10^{-9}\text{ kg}}{1\ \mu\text{g}} \]

    Step 2: Find a conversion factor from m³ to mL

    \[ 1\text{ m}^3 = 1000\text{ L} = 10^{6}\text{ mL} \implies \frac{10^{6}\text{ mL}}{1\text{ m}^3} \]

    Step 3: Multiply by the conversion factors, group numbers and units, and simplify

    \[\begin{align*} 12\ \frac{\mu\text{g}}{\text{mL}} &= 12\ \frac{\mu\text{g}}{\text{mL}} \cdot \frac{10^{-9}\text{ kg}}{1\ \mu\text{g}} \cdot \frac{10^{6}\text{ mL}}{1\text{ m}^3}\\ &= \left(12 \times 10^{-9} \times 10^{6}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{\mu\text{g}}{\text{mL}}\cdot\frac{\text{kg}}{\mu\text{g}}\cdot\frac{\text{mL}}{\text{m}^3}\right)\\ &= 0.012\ \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \end{align*}\]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{0.012\text{ kg/m}^3} \]

    9) A watershed receives 45 mm/year of precipitation over an area of 1,800 km². Find the total annual volume of precipitation in m³/year.

    Solution

    Given

    • Precipitation = 45 mm/year
    • Watershed area = 1,800 km²

    Step 1: Convert precipitation to meters

    \[ 45\text{ mm} = \frac{45}{1000} = 0.045\text{ m} \]

    Step 2: Convert area to square meters

    \[ 1,800\text{ km}^2 = 1,800(1,000)^2 \]

    \[ = 1.8 \times 10^9\text{ m}^2 \]

    Step 3: Calculate the annual precipitation volume

    Volume is calculated as:

    \[ V = P \times A \]

    Substitute the values:

    \[ V = (0.045)(1.8 \times 10^9) \]

    \[ V = 8.1 \times 10^7\text{ m}^3/\text{year} \]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{81,000,000\text{ m}^3/\text{year}} \]

    10) You are combining information from multiple sources to estimate the total annual oil spilled in a watershed, reported by different companies. The following are reported for the year:

    • Company A: 14.6 barrels spilled (with 9.3 barrels recovered)
    • Company B: 692 liters spilled (94% recovered)
    • Company C: 18.1 gallons spilled (0% recovered)

    What is the total unrecovered amount of oil spilled into the watershed that year, in barrels. Use the conversion 1 barrel = 42 gallons = 159 liters and a calculator when needed.

    Solution

    Given

    • Company A: 14.6 barrels spilled, 9.3 barrels recovered
    • Company B: 692 L spilled, 94% recovered
    • Company C: 18.1 gallons spilled, 0% recovered
    • Conversions: 1 barrel = 42 gallons = 159 liters

    Step 1: Company A’s unrecovered oil (already in barrels)

    \[ 14.6 - 9.3 = 5.3\text{ barrels} \]

    Step 2: Company B’s unrecovered oil, converted to barrels

    \[ 692 \times (1 - 0.94) = 41.52\text{ L unrecovered} \]

    \[ 41.52\text{ L} \times \frac{1\text{ barrel}}{159\text{ L}} = 0.26\text{ barrels} \]

    Step 3: Company C’s unrecovered oil, converted to barrels

    \[ 18.1\text{ gal} \times \frac{1\text{ barrel}}{42\text{ gal}} = 0.43\text{ barrels} \]

    Step 4: Add up all three companies’ unrecovered oil

    \[ 5.3 + 0.26 + 0.43 = 5.99\text{ barrels} \]

    Answer

    \[ \boxed{5.99\text{ barrels unrecovered}} \]
