    Solutions: Algebra Problem Set

    Instructions

    These are the solutions to the algebra problem set. Click a solution to expand it and try each problem yourself first!

    Basics

    1) Expand the following expressions:

    1. \((x+2)(3x-4)\)

    2. \((x^2-2y)(x+y^3)\)

    Solution

    1. \((x+2)(3x-4) = 3x^2-4x+6x-8 = 3x^2+2x-8\)

    2. \((x^2-2y)(x+y^3) = x^3+x^2y^3-2yx-2y^4\)

    2) Solve for \(x\) in the following equations:

    1. \(2x-4=3x+12\)

    2. \(-6ax+2b=x-5c\)

    3. \(2x^2+3x-4=0\)

    4. \(x^2-2x-5=0\)

    Solution

    1. \[ \begin{aligned} 2x-4&=3x+12 \\ 2x&=3x+16 &\text{Add 4 to both sides}\\ -x&=16 &\text{Subtract $3x$ from both sides}\\ x&=-16 \end{aligned} \]

    2. \[ \begin{aligned} -6ax+2b&=x-5c \\ -6ax&=x-5c-2b &\text{Subtract $2b$ from both sides} \\ -6ax-x&=-5c-2b &\text{Subtract $x$} \\ x(-6a-1)&=-5c-2b &\text{Factor out $x$}\\ x&=\frac{-5c-2b}{-6a-1} \end{aligned} \]

    3. \[ \begin{aligned} 2x^2+3x-4&=0 \\ x&=\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{3^2-4(2)(-4)}}{2(2)} \\ x_1=\frac{-3+\sqrt{41}}{4}& \qquad x_2=\frac{-3-\sqrt{41}}{4} \\ x_1\approx0.85 & \qquad x_2\approx-2.35 \end{aligned} \]

    4. \[ \begin{aligned} x^2-2x-5&=0 \\ x&=\frac{-(-2)\pm\sqrt{(-2)^2-4(1)(-5)}}{2(1)} \\ x_1=\frac{2+\sqrt{24}}{2}& \qquad x_2=\frac{2-\sqrt{24}}{2} \\ x_1\approx3.45 & \qquad x_2\approx-1.45 \end{aligned} \]

    Applications from your core MESM courses

    Level 1

    3) An organism’s daily energy budget splits intake between growth, metabolism, and reproduction: Intake = Growth + Metabolism + Reproduction. If daily intake is 120 kcal, metabolism is 50 kcal, and reproduction is 15 kcal, solve for Growth.

    Solution

    \(120 = G + 50 + 15 \Rightarrow G = 120 - 65 = 55\) kcal/day.

    4) A tank’s mass balance is Mass in = Mass out + Mass accumulated. If 50 kg entered and 30 kg accumulated, solve for the mass that left.

    Solution

    \(50 = \text{out} + 30 \Rightarrow \text{out} = 20\) kg.

    5) At steady state, a watershed’s water balance is \(0 = P - AET - Q\), where \(P\) is precipitation, \(AET\) is actual evapotranspiration (water returned to the atmosphere), and \(Q\) is streamflow (water leaving via the river). “Steady state” just means none of these are changing over time. If \(P = 900\) mm/y and \(AET = 550\) mm/y, solve for \(Q\).

    Solution

    \(Q = 900 - 550 = 350\) mm/y.

    Level 2

    6) The maximum sustainable yield of a harvested population is \(H = \dfrac{rK}{4}\). If the sustainable harvest is \(H = 80\) individuals/year and the carrying capacity is \(K = 800\), solve for the intrinsic growth rate \(r\).

    Solution

    \(80 = \dfrac{r \cdot 800}{4} = 200r \Rightarrow r = 0.4\) per year.

    7) The aridity index \(AI\) is given by \(AI = P/PET\) where \(P\) is mean annyal precipitation and \(PET\) is potential evapotranspiration. If \(AI = 0.35\) and \(PET = 1400\) mm/y, solve for \(P\).

    Solution

    \(P = 0.35 \times 1400 = 490\) mm/y.

    8) A stock solution is a concentrated “master” solution made at a known, accurate concentration that is meant to be diluted down into weaker “working solutions” for everyday use. How much of a 12% stock solution is needed to make 3 L of a 2% solution?

    Solution

    Dilution equation: \(C_1V_1 = C_2V_2\).

    \(12\% \cdot V_1 = 2\% \cdot 3\text{ L} \Rightarrow V_1 = \dfrac{2 \times 3}{12} = 0.5\) L.

    Scenario

    9) Representative Concentration Pathways (RCP) are climate change scenarios that project future greenhouse gas concentrations in 2100. A new RCP projection indicates that carbon concentrations in the atmosphere will plateau at around 600 ppm. Today’s atmospheric carbon concentration sits at 416 ppm.

    1. If the United States continues to emit proportionally the same amount of carbon (14%), how much carbon will the US emit in 2100 under RCP 4.5? Use the conversion 1 ppm = 2.13 gigaton to give your answer in gigatons.

    2. Let’s say the US decides to pursue an ambitious policy to extract their contribution of carbon from the air. Climatologists model that this policy would have a trajectory of \(f(t)=\frac{-1}{8}t^2+6t+123.83\) where \(t\) is each year and \(f(t)\) is the amount of carbon released by the US. When will the United States eliminate their carbon footprint?

    Solution
    1. Since the US’s share of emissions stays constant at 14%, the US emits 14% of the 600 ppm plateau in 2100:

    \[ \begin{aligned} 600 \times 0.14 &= 84 \text{ ppm}\\ 84 \times 2.13 &= 178.92 \text{ gigaton} \end{aligned} \]

    1. We need the year(s) at which \(f(t) = -\dfrac{1}{8}t^2+6t+123.83 = 0\). Using the quadratic formula with \(a=-\frac18\), \(b=6\), \(c=123.83\):

    \[ \begin{aligned} t &= \frac{-6\pm\sqrt{6^2-4\left(-\frac18\right)(123.83)}}{2\left(-\frac18\right)} \\ t &\approx 63.58 \text{ years, or } t \approx -15.58 \text{ years} \end{aligned} \]

    We only care about the positive year, so the US eliminates its carbon footprint about 63.58 years from now.
